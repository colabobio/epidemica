# Proximity BLE payload, version 1

The bytes two Epidemica devices exchange over Bluetooth when they detect one another. This is a
contract between two independent implementations — Swift and Kotlin — that can never be tested
against each other without hardware, so it is specified here and checked on both sides against the
shared vectors in [`1.0.0.vectors.json`](1.0.0.vectors.json).

Carried as the Herald `PayloadData` for a device. Herald transfers it over a GATT characteristic
read, not in the advertisement itself, so the length limits that apply to advertising data do not
apply here.

## Layout

18 bytes, no padding, no alignment requirements.

| Offset | Size | Field | Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1 | `format_version` | uint8, currently `1` |
| 1 | 16 | `pseudonym` | 16 raw bytes, big-endian, rendered as a canonical lowercase UUID |
| 17 | 1 | `device_class` | uint8: `0` = iOS, `1` = Android, `255` = unknown |

Nothing else. No epidemiological state, no study identifier, no timestamp, no sequence number.

The study is not named on the wire because it does not need to be: devices in different studies use
different BLE service UUIDs and never discover each other. Putting a study identifier in the payload
would make study membership observable to any passive listener, which is a re-identification
surface for no gain.

`device_class` is transmitted rather than inferred because the RSSI-to-distance mapping is
hardware-dependent, and a receiver cannot reliably determine the sender's platform. `255` means the
sender did not know its own class; a receiver records that as null rather than guessing.

## Rules for readers

1. **Reject** a payload shorter than 18 bytes.
2. **Reject** a `format_version` the reader does not implement.
3. **Ignore trailing bytes** beyond offset 17.

Rule 3 is the one that cannot be retrofitted. It is what allows a future version to append fields —
an opaque application extension, say — without making already-deployed builds blind to updated ones.
A reader that rejects unexpected length turns every future format change into a fleet-wide
detection outage, and in a longitudinal study that outage is invisible: the devices simply stop
seeing each other.

Correspondingly, extensions may only ever be **appended**. Offsets 0–17 are frozen for the lifetime
of `format_version` 1.

## Relationship to the Epigames payload

Epigames used a 28-byte layout carrying an 8-byte identifier plus simulated epidemiological state
(`epi`, `clin`, `mod`, `strain`, `ps`, `pi`, `pr`). Those fields are specific to that game and have
no place in a general-purpose research module, so they are removed rather than zeroed. The
identifier also widens from 8 to 16 bytes, so this is a new format, not a truncation of the old one:
the two are not interoperable, and are not intended to be.
